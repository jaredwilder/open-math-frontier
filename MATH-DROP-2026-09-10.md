## Claim classes

The repositories use different kinds of evidence. Do not flatten them into one word such as
"proved."

| class | meaning |
|---|---|
| **KERNEL-VERIFIED** | Lean accepted the stated declaration under the disclosed imports/axioms. This certifies the formal statement, not automatically its fidelity to an English source. |
| **PROVED / EXACT** | A conventional proof or finite exact computation is supplied with its stated scope. |
| **CANDIDATE** | A serious argument or computation is reproduced, but at least one load-bearing dependency remains outside the strongest available seal. |
| **COMPUTATIONAL FRONTIER** | Exhaustive or large-scale search to a stated bound. A frontier is not a proof beyond that bound. |
| **WORKING RECORD** | Ore, attempt logs, unaudited formalizations, dead routes, or proof-engineering material. |
| **RETRACTED / CORRECTED** | A public claim or route that was later found unsound or superseded. It remains public as provenance. |

# I. Headline mathematical results

## `integral-point-sets`

**PROVED / EXACT.** New lower bound for planar integral point sets in general position:

> **d(2,8) > 30000.**

The load-bearing computation proves that the Kreisel–Kurz diameter-minimizing heptagon H1 is
maximal: there is no eighth point at integral distance from all seven vertices. Combined with the
published uniqueness of H1 below diameter 30000, this raises the hereditary lower bound for an
integral octagon. The repository also publishes 394 impossibility certificates, exact small-n
diameter work, seed sweeps, and the height-bound-free extension method.

Repository: https://github.com/jaredwilder/integral-point-sets

## `erdos-theorems`

**KERNEL-VERIFIED.** Curated theorem set: 79 declarations across 20 Lean files and 15 problem
families, each shipped with axiom-footprint and verification material.

The strongest single lane is Erdős #949. The repository includes:

- a sharp finite lemma with the q <= 5 bound and obstruction at 4;
- a kernel-checked countable analogue using Hindman's theorem;
- a full finite-sums strengthening;
- a non-vacuity control;
- additional verified results from #477, #700, #1084 and other campaigns.

The countable result is **not** the full continuum-cardinality Erdős problem. Its value is that it
isolates the countable-to-continuum jump as the remaining obstruction.

Repository: https://github.com/jaredwilder/erdos-theorems

# VII. Release doctrine

1. **No result is upgraded by proximity.** A kernel theorem does not make a neighboring conjecture
   true. A billion-case scan does not prove the next case.
2. **Negative results stay public.** Failed routes, counterexamples, stale claims and retractions
   are part of the mathematical record.
3. **Formal validity and semantic fidelity are separate gates.** `lean-semantic-blades` exists
   precisely because Lean can certify a perfectly proved statement that is not the source problem.
4. **Exact scope travels with the claim.** Finite, asymptotic, conditional, computational,
   countable, continuum, candidate, and source-equivalent are not interchangeable labels.
5. **Pure mathematics is open; load-bearing applied IP is not automatically part of this drop.**
6. **Corrections are append-only in spirit.** If a released claim fails, preserve the original
   provenance and publish the correction rather than erasing the path.
7. **Archive size is not theorem count.** Duplicate families, superseded claims, failed
   formalizations and proof debt stay visible but do not inflate the result ledger.

# IX. Invitation to falsify

If a theorem statement is false, a dependency is missing, a computation does not reproduce, a
novelty claim is already in the literature, or a formal statement mismatches its source problem,
open an issue in the relevant repository with the smallest reproducible objection.

The intended standard of this release is not "trust the author." It is **make the attack surface
public**.
