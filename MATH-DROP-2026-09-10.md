## Release-scale note

An initial GitHub count found **38 public repositories created in 17 hours, 5 minutes, 57 seconds** during the first release window. That is only a lower bound on the release event: large September 10/11 expansions also occurred inside pre-existing repositories, especially `msl-ore-estate`, and many repositories contain numerous independent mathematical objects.

Repository count is therefore a navigation statistic, not a theorem count and not the definition of the drop.

## How to read this map

The release contains several evidence types:

| type | meaning |
|---|---|
| **formal theorem** | Lean accepts the stated theorem under the disclosed axioms/trust boundary. |
| **proved / exact result** | A conventional proof or exact finite computation establishes the stated claim. |
| **candidate result** | A serious reproduced argument still depends on at least one unresolved or externally supplied step. |
| **computational frontier** | An exhaustive or large-scale search reaches an explicit finite boundary. |
| **research archive / corpus** | A heterogeneous collection of proofs, attempts, formalizations, computations, or provenance records. |
| **corrected / retracted result** | A historical claim or route later found unsound or superseded and preserved with its correction. |

The mathematical statement comes first. Verification method, literature status, and correction history are supporting information.

# I. Results that move numbers, ranges, or complete finite families

## `integral-point-sets`

> **`d(2,8) > 30000`** for planar integral point sets in general position.

The previous hereditary lower bound was `d(2,8) >= d(2,7) = 22270`. The key computation proves whole-plane maximality of the Kreisel–Kurz diameter-minimizing heptagon H1; combined with the published uniqueness range below 30000, this gives the new octagon lower bound.

Also included: **394 impossibility certificates**, exact values `d(2,4)=8`, `d(2,5)=73`, `d(2,6)=174`, exact seed sweeps, and a compile-ready preprint.

Repository: https://github.com/jaredwilder/integral-point-sets

## Graham–Alspach sequenceability

Verified finite ranges beyond the published general subset-size range 20:

- `graham-alspach-sequenceability`: `Z_29` subset sizes **21–28** and `Z_31` sizes **21–30**;
- `graham-alspach-extended`: verified ranges for **`Z_37`, `Z_41`, `Z_43`, and `Z_61`**, covering **22,082,109 subsets**;
- `graham-alspach-certificates`: certificate bank including **60,134 `Z_29` rows**, independent Go/Python checkers, deliberately corrupted controls, and deterministic regeneration.

The Lean layer states its `native_decide` verification boundary explicitly.

The active boundary is documented in:

- [`PUBLICATION-FIREWALL.md`](PUBLICATION-FIREWALL.md)

# IX. Editorial and evidence rules

The detailed public writing standard is [`HUMAN-FIRST-EDITORIAL-STANDARD.md`](HUMAN-FIRST-EDITORIAL-STANDARD.md). Its core rules are:

1. **State the strongest established mathematical result first.**
2. **Do not upgrade a result by proximity to a stronger theorem.**
3. **Do not downgrade a result because its parent problem is still larger or unresolved.**
4. **Describe the actual verification method rather than using generic warning language.**
5. **Keep historical novelty separate from mathematical correctness.**
6. **Keep corrections public, but attach them to the claims they correct.**
7. **Report archive counts as archive counts, not theorem counts.**
8. **Use ordinary mathematical language in public prose; preserve internal workflow terminology only where provenance requires it.**

# XI. Invitation to falsify

If a theorem statement is false, a dependency is missing, a computation does not reproduce, a novelty claim collides with prior literature, or a formal statement mismatches its source problem, open an issue in the relevant repository with the smallest reproducible objection.

The standard is simple: **make the mathematical evidence public enough that another person can check it.**
